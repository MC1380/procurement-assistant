# Industrial Bearing — Category Technical Guide

> GENERAL CATEGORY KNOWLEDGE — NOT A PRODUCT-SPECIFIC DATASHEET.
>
> This document provides general educational information about
> industrial bearings. It does not establish verified specifications
> or application suitability for any particular bearing model.

## 1. Product Overview

A bearing is a mechanical component designed to support relative
motion between machine components while reducing friction.

Bearings are commonly used in electric motors, pumps, gearboxes,
fans, conveyors, and industrial machinery.

A suitable bearing must be selected according to load,
speed, operating environment, lubrication, mounting,
and expected service life.

## 2. Working Principle

Bearings support loads and guide relative motion between
rotating or moving components.

Rolling-element bearings use rolling elements between
races to reduce friction.

The rolling elements may be balls or rollers.

The bearing transfers loads between the rotating shaft
and its supporting structure.

Bearing performance depends on mechanical loading,
lubrication, alignment, contamination, temperature,
and operating speed.

## 3. Bearing Types and Configurations

### 3.1 Deep Groove Ball Bearings

Deep groove ball bearings are commonly used for radial loads
and can also support axial loads within their design limits.

They are widely used in electric motors and general machinery.

Their suitability depends on load magnitude, speed,
clearance, sealing, and operating conditions.

### 3.2 Angular Contact Ball Bearings

Angular contact ball bearings are designed to support
combined radial and axial loads.

Their load-carrying behavior depends on contact angle
and bearing arrangement.

### 3.3 Cylindrical Roller Bearings

Cylindrical roller bearings are commonly used where
high radial load capacity is required.

Their suitability depends on design, internal geometry,
and operating conditions.

### 3.4 Tapered Roller Bearings

Tapered roller bearings can support combined radial
and axial loads.

They are often used in applications such as gearboxes,
vehicle wheel assemblies, and industrial machinery.

### 3.5 Spherical Roller Bearings

Spherical roller bearings are designed for heavy radial
loads and can accommodate certain alignment conditions.

Actual misalignment limits must be confirmed from
manufacturer documentation.

## 4. Key Technical Parameters

### 4.1 Bore Diameter

Bore diameter is the internal diameter of the bearing.

It must be compatible with the shaft and the intended fit.

### 4.2 Outside Diameter

Outside diameter describes the external diameter
of the bearing.

It affects housing compatibility and installation space.

### 4.3 Width

Bearing width is an important dimensional parameter
for shaft and housing arrangement.

### 4.4 Bearing Type

Bearing type determines important characteristics
such as load direction, contact geometry, and motion behavior.

### 4.5 Dynamic Load Rating

The basic dynamic load rating is used in bearing life
calculations under specified assumptions.

It must not be confused with the maximum load that a bearing
can safely support in every operating condition.

### 4.6 Static Load Rating

The basic static load rating relates to permanent deformation
considerations under defined conditions.

Static and dynamic load ratings serve different engineering
purposes.

### 4.7 Internal Clearance

Internal clearance is the total possible relative movement
between bearing rings before installation or under defined
measurement conditions.

Operating clearance may change because of fits and temperature.

### 4.8 Limiting and Reference Speed

Limiting speed and reference speed are manufacturer-defined
or method-dependent parameters.

They must not be treated as interchangeable without
understanding their definitions.

A bearing's suitable operating speed depends on its design,
lubrication, load, temperature, and installation.

### 4.9 Sealing Arrangement

Seals and shields help control contamination and lubricant retention.

Common configurations include open bearings, shielded bearings,
and contact-sealed bearings.

The exact meaning of a designation must be verified
against the relevant manufacturer's catalog.

### 4.10 Lubrication

Bearings may use grease or oil lubrication.

Lubricant selection and relubrication requirements depend
on bearing design and application conditions.

## 5. Bearing Selection Guidelines

A bearing should be selected by evaluating the complete
mechanical application.

Important considerations include:

- Shaft and housing dimensions
- Radial and axial loads
- Operating speed
- Required service life
- Bearing arrangement
- Operating temperature
- Lubrication method
- Contamination and moisture exposure
- Vibration and alignment
- Mounting and maintenance requirements
- Available installation space

Dimensional compatibility alone does not establish
bearing suitability.

The selected bearing must satisfy load, speed,
lubrication, environmental, and life requirements together.

## 6. Operating Conditions

Bearing performance is affected by:

- Applied loads
- Rotational speed
- Lubricant type and condition
- Operating temperature
- Shaft and housing fits
- Alignment
- Vibration
- Dust and moisture
- Chemical contamination

Operation outside manufacturer recommendations may
reduce bearing service life or cause failure.

No universal load, speed, or temperature limit can be
assigned to every bearing from category-level knowledge.

## 7. Installation and Maintenance

Correct installation is essential for bearing reliability.

Important considerations include:

- Verify bearing identification and dimensions.
- Inspect shaft and housing conditions.
- Use suitable mounting methods and tools.
- Avoid transmitting installation force through rolling elements.
- Maintain cleanliness during installation.
- Apply the specified lubricant and quantity.
- Follow manufacturer instructions for fits and clearances.
- Monitor temperature, vibration, and abnormal noise.
- Follow appropriate maintenance intervals.

Incorrect mounting can damage bearing components
even before normal operation begins.

## 8. Common Failure Modes

### 8.1 Lubrication Failure

Insufficient, excessive, or unsuitable lubrication
can increase friction and temperature.

### 8.2 Contamination

Dust, water, and foreign particles can damage
rolling surfaces and accelerate wear.

### 8.3 Misalignment

Misalignment can produce abnormal loading
and reduce service life.

### 8.4 Overloading

Loads beyond the bearing's design limits can cause
premature damage or permanent deformation.

### 8.5 Incorrect Installation

Improper mounting, excessive force, or unsuitable fits
can damage bearing components.

### 8.6 Fatigue

Repeated contact stresses can eventually cause
fatigue-related surface damage.

A failure symptom alone does not establish its root cause.
Diagnosis requires application-specific evidence.

## 9. Safety Considerations

Bearing installation and maintenance may involve
rotating machinery, stored mechanical energy,
hot components, and heavy equipment.

Before maintenance:

- Isolate the machine from hazardous energy.
- Follow site lockout/tagout procedures.
- Verify that rotating components have stopped.
- Use suitable tools and protective equipment.
- Follow manufacturer installation instructions.
